🛠️ Assembly

Build it in eight stages

Each stage maps to a module in the curriculum — do them in order and you'll have a fully verified subsystem before moving to the next.

Assemble the chassis & mount the motors

Bolt the four gear motors to the chassis, attach wheels, and mount the caster (if your kit uses one). Route motor wires toward where the driver board will sit.

Wire power, the motor driver & the E-stop

Connect the battery through the emergency-stop switch and a fuse before anything else. Wire the buck converters for the Pi's 5V rail separately from the motor driver's 12V rail — never power the Pi directly off the motor supply.

Flash the Pi & install ROS2

Flash Ubuntu 22.04 (64-bit) to the microSD card, boot the Pi headless over SSH, then install ROS2 Humble and create your workspace (see the Software Setup section below).

Mount & wire the sensors

Mount the IMU close to the robot's center of rotation, attach the camera facing forward, and space the three ultrasonic sensors around the front bumper. Connect the IMU over I²C and the ultrasonics to GPIO.

Bring up the motor driver node & verify /cmd_vel

Write or install a ROS2 driver node that subscribes to /cmd_vel and converts it into PWM signals for each wheel. Confirm with ros2 topic pub /cmd_vel geometry_msgs/Twist that the robot drives, turns, and stops correctly.

Calibrate odometry

Drive the robot exactly 1 meter in a straight line and compare against the encoder-reported distance; adjust your wheel-radius and wheel-base parameters until they match. Do the same for a 360° in-place rotation to calibrate angular odometry.

Mount the LiDAR & build your first map

Mount the RPLidar A1 with a clear 360° view, bring up its ROS2 driver, and confirm /scan looks correct in rviz2. Launch slam_toolbox and drive the robot around a room to build your first map.

Launch Nav2 & add object detection

Load your saved map, bring up the full Nav2 stack, and send a "Nav2 Goal" from rviz2 to watch the global and local planners work together. Finally, launch your object-detection node and confirm detections publish correctly alongside navigation.

⚠️

Safety first: Li-ion packs can be dangerous if punctured, short-circuited, or charged incorrectly — always charge with a balance charger matched to your pack's cell count, never leave batteries charging unattended, and keep a fused, easily reachable emergency-stop switch on every powered test. Run early autonomous tests in an open, obstacle-padded area.

💻 Software Setup

From a blank SD card to a working ROS2 workspace

This mirrors what you'll do inside Module 11 — the same commands, just gathered in one place for reference.

# 1. Install ROS2 Humble on Ubuntu 22.04 (Raspberry Pi)
$ sudo apt update && sudo apt install ros-humble-desktop

# 2. Install navigation & SLAM packages
$ sudo apt install ros-humble-navigation2 \
    ros-humble-nav2-bringup ros-humble-slam-toolbox \
    ros-humble-robot-localization

# 3. Create your workspace
$ mkdir -p ~/robot_ws/src && cd ~/robot_ws
$ colcon build --symlink-install
$ source install/setup.bash

# 4. Bring up your robot's drivers
$ ros2 launch my_robot_bringup robot.launch.py

# 5. Build a map, then navigate
$ ros2 launch slam_toolbox online_async_launch.py
$ ros2 launch nav2_bringup navigation_launch.py \
    map:=~/robot_ws/maps/room.yaml

  • rviz2 & rqt (run from your laptop over the network)
